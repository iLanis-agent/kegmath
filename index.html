Keg math

A keg is bought on a vibe and regretted on a schedule. Somebody guesses the pints, somebody guesses the gas, and the warm keg arrives at the party because nobody counted the hours a fridge needs. Keg math does the counting: gallons and pour size become honest servings, tank pounds become the kegs the gas will push, and the temperature gap becomes the day the chilling has to start. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Pour plan - the keg's gallons and your pour size become the honest count of servings.
  • Gas plan - the CO2 tank's pounds become the half-barrels it will push before it quits.
  • Chill plan - the temperature gap becomes the fridge hours, and the fridge hours become the day you start.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 128 oz per gallon, 1.5 half-barrels per pound of CO2, and 45 minutes of fridge per degree F - starting points, not promises. Your regulator, your fridge and your crowd will amend every number. Foam usually means warm lines or too much pressure, not a bad keg. Know your local rules on keg deposits and serving before the party.
